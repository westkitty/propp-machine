# The Tale of Dunya and the Tallow King

Once, in a village where the rooster crowed backwards, lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

Before leaving, a widowed Tsar warned Dunya: “do not eat the third pancake.” Dunya crossed her heart, and meant it.

Before the porridge cooled, the youngest cousin forgot, then remembered, then did it on purpose.

“Short of the kingdom’s last true sunrise,” sighed King Barley the Bald, “a kingdom is just a field with a fence.”

“Who will go?” cried a widowed Tsar. Even the cat looked away.

Dunya said a short rude prayer and resolved to go.

And Dunya went, it may be near, it may be far; the tale is quick, the road is not. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Dunya chose the road over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

At a crossroads, Dunya met a ferryman with no shadow at a river that had forgotten its other bank. “Answer me this,” it said, and the trees leaned in to listen.

Dunya laughed and refused.

In the very middle of a palace of dripping wax, our Dunya stood before the Tallow King. The waxen king came out, and they fought.

At the cock’s first crow, the waxen king was undone.

By nobody’s leave but her own, Dunya won back the kingdom’s last true sunrise.

The girl went home.

Then, Dunya came back in rags, and nobody knew her.

There was one more task: to build a bridge of crystal in a single night.

By luck and stubbornness, the girl did it before the cock crowed.

Everyone recognised Dunya at last.

Well, Our Dunya bathed in the milk of a white mare and came out glowing like a new kopeck.

Three days later Our Dunya married Princess Vasilka the Unhurried, and there was a feast.
