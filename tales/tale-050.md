# The Tale of Tikhon and the Widow of the Well

They still tell of Tikhon Who Counts Crows in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it: that one, who had never once finished counting.

One day the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet went away to market and did not come back for a long time.

There was a rule in that house, older than the roof: do not open the green door at the end of the passage. The lad nodded, and began to wonder at once.

Forbidden things are heavy; Tikhon Who Counts Crows put it down in exactly the wrong place.

A one-eyed stranger came asking whose child that was. It was the Widow of the Well.

And so the Widow learned all that was needed.

That night, the Widow, a wet grey woman who lived under the bucket, came down the chimney and wrapped up and took Tsarevna Lumina to the bottom of the old well.

The village elder with the brass ear-trumpet called Tikhon and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

Tikhon decided to set things right.

And our Tikhon set off, long or short, high or low; the tale is quick, the road is not. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Tikhon chose the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

A drowned soldier who still kept watch beside a lake as flat as a coin barred the way: “Feed me, or pass on hungry yourself.”

Tikhon found a clever answer.

A drowned soldier who still kept watch opened a sack that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a ball of red thread that knows the way; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown. From under the moss, a drowned soldier who still kept watch brought a ball of red thread that knows the way, and the air smelled of thunder.

Our Tikhon was led down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go as far as the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, our Tikhon stood before the Widow. The Widow set out a board of black and bone, and they played three rounds.

In the fight, the Widow scorched Tikhon on the palm: a mark that would matter later.

And so the Widow was defeated.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

Then, Whistling, which was unwise, Tikhon took the road back to a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it.

Then came a sound of gnawing: the Widow was following, and fast.

A grey wolf in spectacles carried our Tikhon away to safety.

“Who else?,” said the elder brothers Glib and Gleb, and the Tsar clapped, slowly.

The Tsar of the Soup Kingdom set a task: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.

Overnight the task was done, with a little left over.

By his old crooked grin, they knew our Tikhon at last.

The elder brothers Glib and Gleb went red, but everyone had seen.

The Widow was put in a barrel and rolled into the sea, which rolled her back out, ashamed.

Tikhon married Tsarevna Lumina, and there was a feast.
