# The Tale of Ivashka and Count Moth

*Seed 36. Functions: `α γ δ A C ↑ K ↓ U W`. Strict validation: PASS.*

Once, in a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays, lived Ivashka Ninth-Son, who slept on the stove for nineteen winters.

“Only one thing,” said a widowed Tsar, not quite meeting his eyes: “do not let the stove go cold while I am away.” The lad said “Of course”, and began to wonder at once.

Before the porridge cooled, Ivashka Ninth-Son broke the rule, as rules are broken.

Three days later That night Count Moth, a lord made of dust and good manners, carried off Tsarevna Lumina to a castle where every candle was afraid.

“I will go,” said Ivashka.

So the lad left home. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Ivashka chose the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

Before long Tsarevna Lumina was free.

And so, the lad went back the way he had come.

The Count was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Then there was a wedding.
