# The Tale of Tikhon and Bonelace the Deathless

They still tell of Tikhon Who Counts Crows in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves: that one, who had never once finished counting.

Under the window, Bonelace the Deathless counted the family twice.

Well, the youngest cousin could not keep a secret, and the sorcerer wrote it on a fingernail.

Bonelace forged a voice at the smithy.

Tikhon said yes, which was exactly what was wanted.

When the dogs fell silent, the sorcerer, a sorcerer who kept his death somewhere sensible, swept in and snatched away Princess Vasilka the Unhurried to a fortress stitched from bones.

Wouldn’t you know it, a widowed Tsar called our Tikhon and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

“Give me a crust and a blessing, and I will go,” said the lad.

So our Tikhon left home. At the crossroads stone, Tikhon chose the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

By a fire nobody had lit, Tikhon met a hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat under a cart that had no wheels. “Do me a service,” it said, and its eyes were very old.

“That one is easy,” said Tikhon, and the answer was correct, which surprised everyone.

A hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat opened a sack that held three things: a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a ball of red thread that knows the way. A hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat coughed up, rather damply a bone comb that grows forests when thrown. “Use it once.”

Then the lad travelled through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything, and came to the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

There at the heart of the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, Tikhon stood before the sorcerer, a sorcerer who kept his death somewhere sensible. Bonelace challenged Tikhon to a contest of riddles.

And so Bonelace was defeated.

The sunrise was let out of its sack, and Princess Vasilka the Unhurried was free.

Then, the lad came home by the short road, which is never short.

Bonelace flew after the lad.

Tikhon flung the comb, and a forest sprang up thick as a brush, and escaped.

A grey wolf in spectacles gave Tikhon new clothes.

Then there was a wedding.
