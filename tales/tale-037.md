# The Tale of Marfa and the Tallow King

In a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays there lived Marfa Sootfoot, who could whistle in three directions at once.

In time the Tallow King forged a voice at the smithy.

Marfa nodded, which was exactly what was wanted.

That night the waxen king, a monarch who melted a little whenever he lied, carried off Tsarevna Lumina to a palace of dripping wax.

“Must I go myself, with my knees?” cried old father Foma. Even the cat looked away.

“I will go,” said the girl.

Marfa set out along the road. Past the post with the rude carving, our Marfa took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

On the way our Marfa met the Grandmother of Owls in a hut built entirely of locked doors, who said: “First, help me.”

“Find your own crust,” said our Marfa. The Grandmother of Owls watched her go.

Marfa was led down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go as far as the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

The cage swung open, and Tsarevna Lumina stepped out blinking.

Our Marfa went home.

Behind came the waxen king, chasing.

Marfa ran faster than fear, and escaped.

As it happened, the Tsar of the Soup Kingdom tapped the table three times. “Whoever can manage this: to eat a whole oven of bread before dawn.”

Wouldn’t you know it, Overnight the task was done, with a little left over.

By the mark, they knew Marfa at last.

New boots appeared for Marfa.

Justice in that kingdom was strange but thorough: the waxen king was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.
