# The Tale of Dunya and Grandmother Ninetoes

In a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays there lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

There was a rule in that house, older than the roof: do not look into the well after the sun goes down. Dunya crossed her heart, and meant it.

But the house cat forgot the warning at once.

That night Grandmother Ninetoes, a witch who lived in a hut that paced like a dog, carried off Princess Vasilka the Unhurried to her pacing hut.

“Who will go?” cried the hero’s grandmother. The brothers looked at the ceiling.

“I will go,” said the girl.

Dunya set out along the road. Where three ways met, Dunya chose the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

The Grandmother of Owls in a hut built entirely of locked doors held out a cracked cup: “Feed me, or pass on hungry yourself.”

Being polite, Dunya said thank you to the trees.

The Grandmother of Owls opened a sack that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely; boots that walk only forward. In return Dunya received boots that walk only forward.

Dunya was led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything as far as the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

There at the heart of the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, Dunya stood before Ninetoes. Ninetoes came out roaring, and they fought till the grass was flattened, maybe long, maybe short.

With a last blow, Ninetoes melted into a puddle that complained.

Princess Vasilka the Unhurried was free.

In time Dunya went home.

There was one more task: to eat a whole oven of bread before dawn.

With help from the cat, the task was done.

Then Princess Vasilka the Unhurried recognised Dunya.

A cat with nine lives, six already spent gave Dunya new clothes.

As for Ninetoes: Ninetoes was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Then there was a wedding.
