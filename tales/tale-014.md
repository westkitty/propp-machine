# The Tale of Yelena and Old Hungry Mist

Once, in a village where the rooster crowed backwards, lived Yelena Thimble-Eyes, who could see a needle think.

At first frost, old father Foma packed three onions and a prayer and went away, and the house felt hollow.

Meanwhile Old Hungry Mist came sniffing round the house, asking questions.

What one hears at a keyhole is soon known in a lair: the fog learned it all.

Then the Mist came and took the Moonmiller’s daughter away.

Old father Foma rang the cracked bell, and it was Yelena who answered.

Yelena said a short rude prayer and resolved to try.

Yelena set out along the road. Where three ways met, our Yelena took the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

So our Yelena came to the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

At last, the girl stood before the Mist, a fog with teeth and an appetite for names. The girl and the Mist fought in the open field.

The Moonmiller’s daughter pressed a ring into her hand. “So I will know you.”

At the cock’s first crow, the Mist was undone.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

Then our Yelena turned back toward a village where the rooster crowed backwards.

Within sight of the roofs of home, a general with polished boots and no battles sold Yelena to a passing bear and took the prize.

Somehow, Yelena came back into the light, thinner but alive.

Yelena walked home again, angrier than before.

A general with polished boots and no battles sent dogs after Yelena.

Yelena hid in a haystack until the dogs grew bored.

One more trial was set: to eat a whole oven of bread before dawn.

With sheer stubbornness’s help, Yelena did it before the cock crowed.

Then the Moonmiller’s daughter recognised Yelena.

Justice in that kingdom was strange but thorough: the Mist was put in a barrel and rolled into the sea, which rolled him back out, ashamed.

Then there was a wedding.
