# The Tale of Ivashka and Zmei Pickleback

They still tell of Ivashka Ninth-Son in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it: the one, who slept on the stove for nineteen winters.

Before leaving, the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet warned Ivashka: “do not let the stove go cold while I am away.” The lad crossed his heart, and meant it.

Somewhere a door creaked that had no hinges, and the youngest cousin could not help looking.

Zmei Pickleback came in disguise, holding out a sweet cake.

Ivashka took the cake and fell asleep.

Then Pickleback came and took Princess Vasilka the Unhurried away.

The village elder with the brass ear-trumpet wept loudly in the square, and the lad heard.

Now Ivashka tightened his belt and resolved to set it right.

So Ivashka left home. Where three ways met, the lad chose the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

Before long a hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat under a cart that had no wheels asked for a name and a kindness: “Feed me, or pass on hungry yourself.”

“Find your own crust,” said our Ivashka. A hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat watched him go.

So Ivashka came to the Copper Kingdom under the earth, having gone down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

Princess Vasilka the Unhurried was free.

Then the lad turned back toward a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it.

Then came a sound of gnawing: Pickleback was coming after them.

That same week Ivashka ran faster than fear, and Pickleback had to go round, grumbling.

On the way home, the elder brothers Glib and Gleb sold our Ivashka to a passing bear and took the prize.

With help from sheer stubbornness, Ivashka climbed free, cross but whole.

Ivashka came home a second time.

The elder brothers Glib and Gleb claimed the deed for themselves.

As it happened, King Barley the Bald stroked his beard. “Whoever can manage this: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.”

The task was done by morning.

Then Princess Vasilka the Unhurried recognised Ivashka.

Not long after, the lie of the elder brothers Glib and Gleb came out.

Not long after, Ivashka married Princess Vasilka the Unhurried, and there was a feast.
