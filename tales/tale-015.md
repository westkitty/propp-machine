# The Tale of Dunya and Count Moth

They still tell of Dunya of the Leaky Bucket in a village where the rooster crowed backwards: the one, whose bucket leaked only lies.

“Only one thing,” said old father Foma, not quite meeting her eyes: “do not answer if someone calls your name from the forest.” Our Dunya nodded, already wondering.

Forbidden things are heavy; Dunya of the Leaky Bucket could not stop carrying this one.

Not long after, Count Moth, a lord made of dust and good manners, stole a feather of the Ember-Bird.

Old father Foma wept loudly in the square, and the girl heard.

“I will go,” said the girl.

With the crows for company, Dunya went where her feet pointed. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Dunya took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

By the gift’s doing, our Dunya recovered a feather of the Ember-Bird.

Then our Dunya turned back toward a village where the rooster crowed backwards.

Within sight of the roofs of home, Cousin Smarm the water-carrier pushed the girl down a well and went home with the credit.

A hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat under a cart that had no wheels barred the way: “Feed me, or pass on hungry yourself.”

Being kind, Dunya said thank you to the trees.

A hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat opened a sack that held three things: a ball of red thread that knows the way; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; boots that walk only forward. That same week a hedgehog wearing a bishop’s hat gave our Dunya a ball of red thread that knows the way.

Sheer stubbornness found Dunya and carried her by back ways.

Somehow, Dunya came back into the light, cross but whole.

Our Dunya walked home again, angrier than before.

Cousin Smarm the water-carrier heard of it and came running.

A kind miller turned our Dunya into a sack of flour for an afternoon.

Unremembered, Dunya took work in the royal kitchen and waited.

Now Dunya stepped through sheer stubbornness’s ear and out the other and came out handsome.

Then there was a wedding.
