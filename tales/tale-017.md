# The Tale of Ivashka and Old Hungry Mist

Once, in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves, lived Ivashka Ninth-Son, who slept on the stove for nineteen winters.

On the eve of the fair, the hero’s grandmother took a staff and went away, and the house felt hollow.

“Mind this,” said the hero’s grandmother, not quite meeting his eyes: “do not answer if someone calls your name from the forest.” Ivashka said “Of course”, already wondering.

In time But Ivashka Ninth-Son forgot the warning at once.

Something was missing the shape of the kingdom’s last true sunrise, and the Tsar of the Soup Kingdom could not sleep for it.

The news went round, and at last it reached our Ivashka.

“I will go,” said Ivashka.

The lad set out along the road. Where three ways met, the lad took the road that went over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

At a crossroads, our Ivashka met a ferryman with no shadow at a river that had forgotten its other bank. “Do me a service,” it said, and its eyes were very old.

“Ask me a harder one,” said Ivashka, and answered.

A ferryman with no shadow opened a sack that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; boots that walk only forward; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely. From under the moss, a ferryman with no shadow brought a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm, and somewhere a bell rang.

Ivashka was led over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud as far as the Thrice-Tenth Sea.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

Then Ivashka turned back toward a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves.

Old Hungry Mist flew after Ivashka.

When the teeth were an inch away, the cat hid him in a cuckoo’s egg.

Within sight of the roofs of home, Cousin Smarm the water-carrier cut the rope and left Ivashka in the pit and went home with the credit.

There sat a ferryman with no shadow at a river that had forgotten its other bank, who asked Ivashka a question.

Ivashka helped without complaint.

A ferryman with no shadow opened a sack that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; boots that walk only forward; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely. Out of a knot in the wood, a ferryman with no shadow brought a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm, and the air smelled of thunder.

Somehow, Ivashka came back into the light, cross but whole.

Our Ivashka came home a second time.

“It was us,” said Cousin Smarm the water-carrier, and the Tsar clapped, slowly.

There was one more task: to build a bridge of crystal in a single night.

With the cat’s help, Ivashka did it before the cock crowed.

By the deed, they knew Ivashka at last.

Cousin Smarm the water-carrier was exposed.

New boots appeared for Ivashka.

And the Mist was put in a barrel and rolled into the sea, which rolled him back out, ashamed.

Ivashka married Tsarevna Lumina, and there was a feast.
