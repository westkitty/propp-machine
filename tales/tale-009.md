# The Tale of Kuzma and Count Moth

Past three rivers and a rumour, in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves, there once lived Kuzma Crookedcap, whose hat pointed always toward trouble.

There was a rule in that house, older than the roof: do not answer if someone calls your name from the forest. Our Kuzma nodded very seriously, and meant it.

Of course the youngest cousin did exactly what was forbidden.

There was a gap in the world the shape of a bride who could laugh, and the Tsaritsa of Nine Rivers talked of nothing else.

“I will go,” said Kuzma.

And Kuzma went, it may be near, it may be far; the tale is quick, the road is not. At the crossroads stone, Kuzma chose the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

There sat a ferryman with no shadow at a river that had forgotten its other bank, who asked Kuzma a question.

“Ask me a harder one,” said our Kuzma, and answered.

A ferryman with no shadow opened a sack that held three things: a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely; a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a jar of borrowed thunder. A ferryman with no shadow gave Kuzma a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm.

Then Kuzma travelled through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything, and came to the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

There at the heart of the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, Kuzma stood before Count Moth. Kuzma tricked the Count instead of fighting.

And so the Count was defeated.

Then, everything that had been taken was put right.

Then Kuzma turned back toward a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves.

A little wooden doll that ate porridge gave Kuzma new clothes.

And the Count was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.

The lad married Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands, and there was a feast.
