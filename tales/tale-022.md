# The Tale of Olya and Count Moth

In a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays there lived Olya Ashpocket, who kept a live coal in her apron for luck.

“Little dove,” said Count Moth, offering a pie that steamed in the wrong direction, “open the door.”

Our Olya took the cake and fell asleep.

Then the Count came and took Princess Vasilka the Unhurried away.

The news went round, and at last it reached the girl.

Three days later Olya decided to set things right.

With the crows for company, Olya took the road. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, our Olya took the road over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

On the way our Olya met an old woman knitting fog on a stump at the edge of the road, who said: “First, help me.”

Olya helped without complaint.

An old woman knitting fog unfolded a rag that held three things: a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a jar of borrowed thunder. An old woman knitting fog pulled from a sleeve a jar of borrowed thunder. “Do not thank me yet.”

Then our Olya travelled over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud, and came to the Thrice-Tenth Sea.

At last, our Olya stood before the Count. The Count proposed a contest.

The Count was beaten.

The sunrise was let out of its sack, and Princess Vasilka the Unhurried stepped out blinking.

Then the girl turned back toward a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays.

Behind came the Count, chasing.

Olya unstopped the jar, and borrowed thunder rolled out, very loud and slightly apologetic, and the Count had to go round, grumbling.

Three days later unremembered, Olya took work in the royal kitchen and waited.

The elder brothers Glib and Gleb said that they had done everything.

The court demanded proof: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.

With the gift’s help, our Olya did it before the cock crowed.

Before long Princess Vasilka the Unhurried saw the mark and cried, “This is the one!”

The elder brothers Glib and Gleb was exposed.

A crown that fitted, for once were waiting for Olya.

Nobody was sorry: the Count was put in a barrel and rolled into the sea, which rolled him back out, ashamed.

Olya married Princess Vasilka the Unhurried, and there was a feast.
