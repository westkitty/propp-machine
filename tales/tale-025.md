# The Tale of Tikhon and the Widow of the Well

They still tell of Tikhon Who Counts Crows in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it: that one, who had never once finished counting.

One day the hero’s grandmother went away to market and did not come back for a long time.

Now King Barley the Bald longed for the kingdom’s last true sunrise, and grew thin with longing.

“Must I go myself, with my knees?” cried the hero’s grandmother. Only Tikhon stood up.

The lad tightened his belt and resolved to try.

With the crows for company, the lad went where his feet pointed. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, the lad took the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

There sat the Grandmother of Owls in a hut built entirely of locked doors, who asked Tikhon a question.

Being polite, Tikhon bowed low.

The Grandmother of Owls opened a sack that held three things: a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; boots that walk only forward. In return our Tikhon received a bone comb that grows forests when thrown.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, Tikhon stood before the Widow of the Well, a wet grey woman who lived under the bucket. Then, the Widow proposed a contest.

And so the Widow was defeated.

By the gift’s doing, Tikhon won back the kingdom’s last true sunrise.

Then Tikhon turned back toward a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it.

Justice in that kingdom was strange but thorough: the Widow was put in a barrel and rolled into the sea, which rolled her back out, ashamed.

Naturally, the lad married Princess Vasilka the Unhurried, and there was a feast.
