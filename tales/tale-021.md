# The Tale of Ivashka and Old Hungry Mist

Nobody in a village where the rooster crowed backwards expected much of Ivashka Ninth-Son, who slept on the stove for nineteen winters; our Ivashka least of all.

A polite stranger came asking what the house was afraid of. It was Old Hungry Mist.

And so the Mist learned all that was needed.

The Mist disguised himself as a pedlar of ribbons.

Well, Ivashka said yes, which was the worst thing to say.

That night the fog, a fog with teeth and an appetite for names, carried off Princess Vasilka the Unhurried to the marsh where the road gave up.

The news went round, and at last it reached Ivashka.

And Ivashka went, it may be near, it may be far; the tale is quick, the road is not. Past the post with the rude carving, our Ivashka chose the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

There sat a birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms, who asked Ivashka a question.

Ivashka mended the cup with a spit and a song, though it took all night.

A birch tree with a terrible cough unfolded a rag that held three things: boots that walk only forward; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm. A birch tree with a terrible cough coughed up, rather damply a bone comb that grows forests when thrown. “Do not thank me yet.”

Well, Our Ivashka was led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything as far as the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

In the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, the lad met a king in want of a son-in-law with nerve.

Up out of the dark, Ivashka came back into the light, thinner but alive.

The lad arrived home, but nobody knew him.

Not long after, a general with polished boots and no battles claimed the deed for himself.

There was one more task: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.

With help from the judge-goose, the task was done.

Everyone recognised our Ivashka at last.

A general with polished boots and no battles tried to slip out by the scullery, but everyone had seen.

And the Mist was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Ivashka and Princess Vasilka the Unhurried ruled together, and are living still, if they have not died.
