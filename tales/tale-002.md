# The Tale of Agafya and Zmei Pickleback

In a village where the rooster crowed backwards there lived Agafya Last-of-Eleven, whom nobody remembered to name until she was six.

“Only one thing,” said the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet, not quite meeting her eyes: “do not answer if someone calls your name from the forest.” Agafya nodded very seriously, and meant it.

Within the hour, the youngest cousin forgot, then remembered, then did it on purpose.

“Little dove,” said Zmei Pickleback, wearing a grandmother’s voice like a shawl, “take a bite.”

The girl believed every word.

Then, That night Pickleback, a salt-green serpent with three heads and one opinion, carried off Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands to a cave that smelled of brine.

Now the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet rang the cracked bell, and it was our Agafya who answered.

Agafya decided to set things right.

The girl set out along the road. Where three ways met, the girl took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

Riding the wind’s spare horse, Agafya went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go, until the road ran out at the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, Agafya stood before Pickleback. Agafya played a trick on Pickleback.

Pickleback was beaten fair, which was the bitter part, and went to pieces, quite literally.

As it happened, the cage swung open, and Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands stepped out blinking.

Meanwhile, Agafya went home.

Three days later Pickleback flew after the girl.

A goose who used to be a judge carried the girl away to safety.

Within sight of the roofs of home, Cousin Smarm the water-carrier cut the rope and left Agafya in the pit and took the prize.

On the way Agafya met a birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms, who said: “First, help me.”

Agafya helped without complaint.

A birch tree with a terrible cough unfolded a rag that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; boots that walk only forward. Then, a birch tree with a terrible cough gave Agafya a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm.

A stray thread of the road led Agafya on.

Up out of the dark, the girl climbed free, thinner but alive.

Before long the girl arrived home, but nobody knew her.

The Tsaritsa of Nine Rivers set a task: to find which of twelve identical doves was the princess.

Before the candle was half gone the task was done, neatly.

Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands saw the mark and cried, “It is you!”

Agafya bathed in the milk of a white mare and came out glowing like a new kopeck.

Agafya married Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands, and there was a feast.
