# The Tale of Dunya and Grandmother Ninetoes

Nobody in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves expected much of Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies; the neighbours even less.

Under the window, Grandmother Ninetoes counted the family twice.

And so Ninetoes learned all that was needed.

Ninetoes put on a kindly face and a borrowed voice.

Our Dunya nodded, which was exactly what was wanted.

Ninetoes stole the egg in which a certain death was kept, and the cows gave sour milk.

The news went round, and at last it reached Dunya.

“I will go,” said Dunya.

So Dunya left home. At the crossroads stone, Dunya took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

Following a hare that was not a hare, Dunya went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go, until the stars changed at the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, Dunya stood before Ninetoes. Our Dunya tricked Ninetoes instead of fighting.

In the struggle Dunya was marked on the cheek.

With a sound like a wet bell, Ninetoes was undone.

By sheer stubbornness’s help, Dunya won back the egg in which a certain death was kept.

With the prize in her cap, the girl took the road back to a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves.

Ninetoes flew after Dunya.

At the last breath, sheer stubbornness hid her in a cuckoo’s egg.

Wouldn’t you know it, Cousin Smarm the water-carrier claimed the deed for himself.

King Barley the Bald set a task: to answer a riddle that had no right answer.

The task was done by morning.

By her old crooked grin, they knew our Dunya at last.

Cousin Smarm the water-carrier was exposed.

A cat with nine lives, six already spent gave Dunya new clothes.

Justice in that kingdom was strange but thorough: Ninetoes was put in a barrel and rolled into the sea, which rolled her back out, ashamed.

Dunya married Princess Vasilka the Unhurried, and there was a feast.
