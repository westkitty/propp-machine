# The Tale of Olya and Grandmother Ninetoes

Past three rivers and a rumour, in a village where the rooster crowed backwards, there was born Olya Ashpocket, who kept a live coal in her apron for luck.

Then old father Foma fell ill and died, and Olya was left in charge.

Grandmother Ninetoes sent a crow to find out where things were kept.

Of course the weathervane told the stranger everything, and Ninetoes smiled with too many teeth.

“Dear child,” said Ninetoes, wearing a grandmother’s voice like a shawl, “open the door.”

The girl took the cake and fell asleep.

When the dogs fell silent, Ninetoes, a witch who lived in a hut that paced like a dog, seeped under the door and wrapped up and took Princess Vasilka the Unhurried to her pacing hut.

“Is there anyone not busy?” cried old father Foma. Only Olya stood up.

Olya put on her other boot and resolved to go.

And Olya set off, long or short, high or low; the tale is quick, the road is not. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Olya took the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

There sat a birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms, who asked Olya a question.

Olya answered politely.

A birch tree with a terrible cough opened a sack that held three things: a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely; boots that walk only forward; a jar of borrowed thunder. A birch tree with a terrible cough gave Olya a jar of borrowed thunder.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, the girl stood before Ninetoes. Olya hid in a cream jug, and Ninetoes fell for it.

Princess Vasilka the Unhurried pressed a ring into her hand. “So I will know you.”

Of course Ninetoes was beaten.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

Olya went home.

Ninetoes rolled like a fog after Olya, shrieking.

Olya unstopped the jar, and borrowed thunder rolled out, very loud and slightly apologetic, and escaped.

Princess Vasilka the Unhurried saw the ribbon and cried, “It is you!”

Now a crown that fitted, for once appeared for the girl.

Our Olya married Princess Vasilka the Unhurried, and there was a feast.
