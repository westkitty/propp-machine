# The Tale of Tikhon and Zmei Pickleback

Nobody in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it expected much of Tikhon Who Counts Crows, who had never once finished counting; Tikhon least of all.

One day the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet went away to market and did not come back for a long time.

Before leaving, the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet warned Tikhon: “do not eat the third pancake.” The lad crossed his heart, and meant it.

Of course a small sound came from the green dark, and the youngest cousin could not help looking.

Meanwhile, Zmei Pickleback, a salt-green serpent with three heads and one opinion, stole the kingdom’s last true sunrise.

“Is there anyone not busy?” cried the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet. The brothers looked at the ceiling.

Three days later Our Tikhon tightened his belt and resolved to try.

Tikhon set out along the road. Where three ways met, our Tikhon took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

The Grandmother of Owls in a hut built entirely of locked doors held out a cracked cup: “First a task, then a road.”

“Ask me a harder one,” said the lad, and the answer was correct, which surprised everyone.

The Grandmother of Owls unfolded a rag that held three things: a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a ball of red thread that knows the way; a jar of borrowed thunder. In return our Tikhon received a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake.

On the back of the judge-goose, Tikhon went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go, until the road ran out at the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

In the very middle of a cave that smelled of brine, Tikhon stood before Pickleback. Not long after, Pickleback came out, and they fought.

Pickleback was beaten.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

With the prize in his cap, our Tikhon turned for home.

Unremembered, Tikhon took work minding geese and waited.

Tikhon married the Moonmiller’s daughter, and there was a feast.
