# The Tale of Dunya and Count Moth

Where the map gives up and the geese take over, in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves, there lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

Then old father Foma went off to sell a cow and a silence, and Dunya was left alone with the stove.

“Only one thing,” said old father Foma, in the voice reserved for funerals: “do not look into the well after the sun goes down.” Dunya said “Of course”, already wondering.

A small sound came from the green dark, and Dunya of the Leaky Bucket could not help looking.

Count Moth sent a crow to find out where things were kept.

A chattering neighbour told the Count everything.

And But the land lacked the egg in which a certain death was kept, and without it nothing was right.

Old father Foma called the girl and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

Meanwhile, Dunya tightened her belt and resolved to go.

So Dunya left home. Where three ways met, the girl took the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

An old woman knitting fog on a stump at the edge of the road asked for a name and a kindness: “Feed me, or pass on hungry yourself.”

Dunya was rude and walked on.

At last, Dunya stood before the lord, a lord made of dust and good manners. The lord set out a board of black and bone, and they matched wits three rounds.

The lord was beaten.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

Whistling, which was unwise, Dunya turned for home.

On the way home, the elder brothers Glib and Gleb cut the rope and left Dunya in the pit and took the prize.

With help from sheer stubbornness, Dunya came back into the light, cross but whole.

The girl walked home again, angrier than before.

Dunya arrived home, but nobody knew her.

The Tsar of the Soup Kingdom set a task: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.

With help from sheer stubbornness, the task was done.

Then Tsarevna Lumina recognised Dunya.

Then there was a wedding.
