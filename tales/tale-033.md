# The Tale of Prokop and Count Moth

They still tell of Prokop Half-Asleep in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves: the one, who was brave only before breakfast.

Then the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet set off on business and left Prokop alone.

“Dear child,” said Count Moth, offering a pie that steamed in the wrong direction, “come closer.”

Wouldn’t you know it, the lad nodded, which was the worst thing to say.

At the stroke of nobody’s clock, the Count, a lord made of dust and good manners, swept in and snatched away the Moonmiller’s daughter to a castle where every candle was afraid.

“Give me a crust and a blessing, and I will go,” said the lad.

And Prokop went, long or short, high or low; the tale is quick, the road is not. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, our Prokop took the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

By a fire nobody had lit, Prokop met a drowned soldier who still kept watch beside a lake as flat as a coin. “Do me a service,” it said, and its eyes were very old.

Prokop answered politely.

A drowned soldier who still kept watch opened a sack that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; boots that walk only forward. Out of a knot in the wood, a drowned soldier who still kept watch brought boots that walk only forward, and the air smelled of thunder.

Then our Prokop travelled down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go, and came to the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

And so, our Prokop went back the way he had come.

The Count flew after Prokop.

When the teeth were an inch away, the rain-horse hid him in a cuckoo’s egg.

Before long a general with polished boots and no battles said that he had done everything.

The Moonmiller tapped the table three times. “Whoever can manage this: to find which of twelve identical doves was the princess.”

With help from the rain-horse, the task was done.

Everyone recognised Prokop at last.

A general with polished boots and no battles tried to slip out by the scullery, but the goose remembered.

The Count was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Prokop and the Moonmiller’s daughter ruled together, and lived long.
