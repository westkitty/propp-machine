# The Tale of Olya and the Widow of the Well

In a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays there lived Olya Ashpocket, who kept a live coal in her apron for luck.

At first frost, the hero’s grandmother took a staff and went away, and the house felt watched.

“Kind soul,” said the Widow of the Well, wearing a grandmother’s voice like a shawl, “open the door.”

That same week Our Olya yawned, which was exactly what was wanted.

The Widow, a wet grey woman who lived under the bucket, stole the kingdom’s last true sunrise.

Now the hero’s grandmother called Olya and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

Our Olya decided to set things right.

Meanwhile, Olya set out along the road. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, the girl took the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

By a fire nobody had lit, Olya met a birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms. “Do me a service,” it said, and its eyes were very old.

Not long after, Olya helped without complaint.

A birch tree with a terrible cough unfolded a rag that held three things: a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely; boots that walk only forward; a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm. From under the moss, a birch tree with a terrible cough brought a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely, and the air smelled of thunder.

Riding the wind’s spare horse, our Olya went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything, until the road ran out at the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

There at the heart of the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, the girl stood before the Widow. The girl mended the villain’s shadow badly on purpose, and the Widow fell for it.

The Widow was beaten.

Olya found the kingdom’s last true sunrise again.

The girl went home.

The Widow scuttled on a hundred borrowed legs after our Olya, singing a lullaby, which was worse.

A grey wolf in spectacles carried Olya away to safety.

So our Olya married the Moonmiller’s daughter, and the bells were rung so hard that one ran away.
