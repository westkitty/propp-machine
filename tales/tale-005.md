# The Tale of Marfa and Bonelace the Deathless

In a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it there lived Marfa Sootfoot, who could whistle in three directions at once.

One day the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet went away to market and did not come back for a long time.

Meanwhile Bonelace the Deathless came sniffing round the house, asking questions.

The weathervane told the stranger everything, and Bonelace wrote it on a fingernail.

Bonelace stole the kingdom’s last true sunrise, and every candle in the land burned blue.

“Who will go?” cried the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet. Even the cat looked away.

“I have nothing better to do, and I will go,” said our Marfa.

And Marfa set off, it may be near, it may be far; the tale is quick, the road is not. At the crossroads stone, Marfa took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

A birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms held out a cracked cup: “Feed me, or pass on hungry yourself.”

“Ask me a harder one,” said Marfa, and the answer was correct, which surprised everyone.

A birch tree with a terrible cough opened a sack that held three things: a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a jar of borrowed thunder. From under the moss, a birch tree with a terrible cough brought a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake, and the air smelled of thunder.

So our Marfa came to the Copper Kingdom under the earth, having gone down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, Marfa stood before Bonelace, a sorcerer who kept his death somewhere sensible. Marfa and Bonelace fought in the open field.

At the cock’s first crow, Bonelace burst into moths.

Marfa found the kingdom’s last true sunrise again.

After that, Marfa went back the way she had come.

Not long after, the sorcerer flew in a mortar after Marfa, shrieking.

When the teeth were an inch away, the skull turned her into a spoon nobody looks at.

On the way home, the Captain of Mild Courage sold the girl to a passing bear and took the prize.

With help from the skull, Marfa came back into the light, cross but whole.

Marfa walked home again, angrier than before.

The Captain of Mild Courage sent dogs after our Marfa.

A kind miller turned Marfa into a sack of flour for an afternoon.

Then, the Captain of Mild Courage claimed the deed for himself.

The Captain of Mild Courage tried to slip out by the scullery, but everyone had seen.

And Bonelace was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Marfa married Tsarevna Lumina, and there was a feast.
