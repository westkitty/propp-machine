# The Tale of Dunya and Grandmother Ninetoes

Once, in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves, lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

Meanwhile Grandmother Ninetoes came sniffing round the house, asking questions.

And so Ninetoes learned all that was needed.

That night Ninetoes, a witch who lived in a hut that paced like a dog, carried off the Moonmiller’s daughter to her pacing hut.

That same week Old father Foma called our Dunya and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

The girl put on her other boot and resolved to go.

So Dunya left home. Where three ways met, Dunya took the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

Then Dunya travelled through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything, and came to the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

At last, Dunya stood before Ninetoes. “Fighting is for peasants,” said Ninetoes. “Let us weigh ourselves on the great scales.”

With a last blow, Ninetoes melted into a puddle that complained.

Then, the Moonmiller’s daughter was free.

And so, Dunya went back the way she had come.

Behind came Ninetoes, chasing.

A little wooden doll that ate porridge carried Dunya away to safety.

One more trial was set: to eat a whole oven of bread before dawn.

Overnight the task was done, neatly.

By the mark, they knew Dunya at last.

Then, a little wooden doll that ate porridge gave the girl new clothes.

And Ninetoes: Ninetoes was tied to a mule’s tail and taken to a very long sermon.

So Dunya married the Moonmiller’s daughter, and I was there, and drank mead; it ran down my beard but never reached my mouth.
