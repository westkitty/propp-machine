# The Tale of Kuzma and the Tallow King

Nobody in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves expected much of Kuzma Crookedcap, whose hat pointed always toward trouble; the cat least of all.

One day the hero’s grandmother went away to market and did not come back for a long time.

There was a rule in that house, older than the roof: do not open the green door at the end of the passage. The lad nodded very seriously, and meant it.

But the house cat forgot the warning at once.

Then the Tallow King came and stole the water of life and the water of death, and the whole land went dim.

The news went round, and at last it reached Kuzma.

Kuzma put on his other boot and resolved to set it right.

Kuzma set out along the road. Where three ways met, Kuzma took the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

By a fire nobody had lit, Kuzma met a ferryman with no shadow at a river that had forgotten its other bank. “Answer me this,” it said, and its eyes were very old.

Kuzma solved the puzzle quickly.

A ferryman with no shadow unfolded a rag that held three things: a ball of red thread that knows the way; boots that walk only forward; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown. From under the moss, a ferryman with no shadow brought boots that walk only forward, and the air smelled of thunder.

Kuzma was led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything as far as the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

In the very middle of a palace of dripping wax, the lad stood before the waxen king. Meanwhile, the waxen king came out roaring, and they fought till the grass was flattened, while the crows kept score.

And so the waxen king was defeated.

Our Kuzma found the water of life and the water of death again.

Meanwhile, Our Kuzma went home.

Then came a sound of gnawing: the waxen king was in pursuit.

Wouldn’t you know it, Kuzma stamped in the forward-boots and could not be turned back, and escaped.

Unremembered, Kuzma took work in the royal kitchen and waited.

The elder brothers Glib and Gleb claimed the deed for themselves.

The court demanded proof: to find which of twelve identical doves was the princess.

By luck and stubbornness, our Kuzma did it, and yawned.

Everyone recognised Kuzma at last.

The elder brothers Glib and Gleb tried to slip out by the scullery, but the goose remembered.

Then there was a wedding.
