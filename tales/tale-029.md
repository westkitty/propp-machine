# The Tale of Prokop and Count Moth

Beyond the thrice-ninth land, in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it, there once lived Prokop Half-Asleep, who was brave only before breakfast.

Before leaving, the hero’s grandmother warned the lad: “do not let the stove go cold while I am away.” The lad said “Of course”, and began to wonder at once.

Of course Prokop Half-Asleep did exactly what was forbidden.

Before cockcrow there was a scorched place where Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands had been. Count Moth had come.

The hero’s grandmother called Prokop and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

So the lad left home. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Prokop took the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

A birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms held out a cracked cup: “First a task, then a road.”

Well, Our Prokop answered politely.

A birch tree with a terrible cough unfolded a rag that held three things: a ball of red thread that knows the way; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely. A birch tree with a terrible cough gave Prokop a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, the lad stood before the lord, a lord made of dust and good manners. The Count came out, and they fought.

The lord was beaten.

Then, the sunrise was let out of its sack, and Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands laughed for the first time in a year.

Then Prokop turned back toward a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it.

“Who else?,” said the Captain of Mild Courage, and the court believed it.

There was one more task: to find which of twelve identical doves was the princess.

Before the candle was half gone the task was done, neatly.

Everyone recognised the lad at last.

The Captain of Mild Courage was exposed.

The Count was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Then there was a wedding.
