Past three rivers and a rumour, in a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays, there once lived Savva the Turnip-Brave, who once outstared a turnip.

A widowed Tsar sang it like a lullaby: do not answer if someone calls your name from the forest. The lad crossed his heart, and meant it.

Within the hour, the youngest cousin did it anyway.

A polite stranger came asking the way to the well. It was Count Moth.

And so the lord learned all that was needed.

Before cockcrow there was a cold place where Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands had been. The Count had come.

The news went round, and at last it reached our Savva.

As it happened, the lad said a short rude prayer and resolved to set it right.

And Savva went, long or short, high or low; the tale is quick, the road is not. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, our Savva chose the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

There sat a birch tree with a terrible cough in a clearing full of polite mushrooms, who asked Savva a question.

Our Savva helped without complaint.

A birch tree with a terrible cough opened a sack that held three things: boots that walk only forward; a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely. From under the moss, a birch tree with a terrible cough brought a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely, and the air smelled of thunder.

Our Savva was led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything as far as the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

The spell came unstitched, and Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands was free.

Before long Savva went home.

The Count flew in a mortar after Savva, shrieking.

A little wooden doll that ate porridge carried our Savva away to safety.

As it happened, Savva came back as a cook’s boy, and the dog alone wagged.

Wouldn’t you know it, the Captain of Mild Courage said that he had done everything.

The court demanded proof: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.

Overnight the task was done, neatly.

As it happened, everyone recognised Savva at last.

The truth came out like a cat from a sack: the Captain of Mild Courage had spent the whole time hiding.

Our Savva washed and became handsome.

Justice in that kingdom was strange but thorough: the Count was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.
