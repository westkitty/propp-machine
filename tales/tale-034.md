# The Tale of Dunya and Zmei Pickleback

Once, in a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves, lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

Then a widowed Tsar set off on business and left Dunya alone.

“Only one thing,” said a widowed Tsar, in the voice reserved for funerals: “do not look into the well after the sun goes down.” Dunya crossed her heart, and meant it.

But the youngest cousin forgot the warning at once.

Before cockcrow there was a cold place where Tsarevna Lumina had been. Zmei Pickleback had come.

A widowed Tsar beat on a pan, and Dunya heard.

Dunya put on her other boot and resolved to go.

So Dunya left home. At the crossroads stone, our Dunya took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

On the way Dunya met the Grandmother of Owls in a hut built entirely of locked doors, who said: “First, help me.”

Dunya answered politely.

The Grandmother of Owls unfolded a rag that held three things: a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely. From under the moss, the Grandmother of Owls brought a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake, and the air smelled of thunder.

Following a hare that was not a hare, the girl went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go, until the stars changed at the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

At last, the girl stood before Pickleback. Dunya whispered the wrong name, and Pickleback fell for it.

And so Pickleback was defeated.

Tsarevna Lumina was free.

Then the girl turned back toward a town whose church bell rang a minute early, out of nerves.

Within sight of the roofs of home, a general with polished boots and no battles sold the girl to a passing bear and went home with the credit.

Somehow, our Dunya climbed free, thinner but alive.

“It was us,” said a general with polished boots and no battles, and the Tsar clapped, slowly.

The Tsar of the Soup Kingdom set a task: to answer a riddle that had no right answer.

With help from the judge-goose, the task was done.

Of course Tsarevna Lumina looked twice and cried, “It is you!”

The lie of a general with polished boots and no battles came out.

Nobody was sorry: Pickleback was tied to a mule’s tail and taken to a very long sermon.

So Dunya married Tsarevna Lumina, with pancakes stacked to the rafters.
