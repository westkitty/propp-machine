# The Tale of Savva and the Tallow King

Once, in a village where the rooster crowed backwards, lived Savva the Turnip-Brave, who once outstared a turnip.

At first frost, a widowed Tsar took a staff and went away, and the house felt hollow.

Then the Tallow King came and took Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands away.

“Is there anyone not busy?” cried a widowed Tsar. Even the cat looked away.

“I will go,” said Savva.

Savva set out along the road. Where three ways met, Savva took the road down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

On the way the lad met the Grandmother of Owls in a hut built entirely of locked doors, who said: “First, help me.”

Savva solved the puzzle quickly.

The Grandmother of Owls opened a sack that held three things: boots that walk only forward; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a jar of borrowed thunder. Out of a knot in the wood, the Grandmother of Owls brought a jar of borrowed thunder, and the air smelled of thunder.

Following a hare that was not a hare, Savva went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go, until the stars changed at the Copper Kingdom under the earth.

In the very middle of a palace of dripping wax, the lad stood before the waxen king. The waxen king proposed a contest.

In the fight, the waxen king scorched Savva on the palm: a mark like a little moon.

With a last blow, the waxen king burst into moths.

Three days later everything that had been taken was put right.

Then the lad turned back toward a village where the rooster crowed backwards.

Then came a sound of gnawing: the waxen king was coming after them.

Just in time, the skull turned him into a spoon nobody looks at.

The Captain of Mild Courage claimed the deed for himself.

The court demanded proof: to eat a whole oven of bread before dawn.

With the skull’s help, the lad did it, and yawned.

By the mark, they knew Savva at last.

Then, the lie of the Captain of Mild Courage came out.

Naturally, the waxen king was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.

Savva married Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands, and there was a feast.
