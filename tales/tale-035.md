# The Tale of Savva and Bonelace the Deathless

In a village where the rooster crowed backwards there lived Savva the Turnip-Brave, who once outstared a turnip.

When the dogs fell silent, Bonelace the Deathless, a sorcerer who kept his death somewhere sensible, swept in and snatched away Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands to a fortress stitched from bones.

Old father Foma called Savva and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

Savva tightened his belt and resolved to try.

So Savva left home. Where three ways met, Savva chose the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

A ferryman with no shadow at a river that had forgotten its other bank barred the way: “First a task, then a road.”

Savva answered with a question and got it right on the third try.

A ferryman with no shadow unfolded a rag that held three things: a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a jar of borrowed thunder. A ferryman with no shadow gave Savva a jar of borrowed thunder.

Our Savva was led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything as far as the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

In the very middle of a fortress stitched from bones, the lad stood before Bonelace. Before long iron met brine. Our Savva and Bonelace wrestled until the moon came out to watch.

Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands tore a ribbon from her sleeve onto his wrist. “So I will know you.”

Bonelace lost, and went to pieces, quite literally.

Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands was free.

That same week Savva went home.

“It was us,” said a general with polished boots and no battles, and the court believed it.

There was one more task: to answer a riddle that had no right answer.

The task was done by morning.

Then Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands recognised our Savva.

The lie fell over by itself: a general with polished boots and no battles had spent the whole time hiding.

And Bonelace: Bonelace was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Then there was a wedding.
