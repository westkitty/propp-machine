# The Tale of Ivashka and Old Hungry Mist

Nobody in a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays expected much of Ivashka Ninth-Son, who slept on the stove for nineteen winters; the cat least of all.

Before leaving, the hero’s grandmother warned Ivashka: “do not eat the third pancake.” Ivashka crossed his heart, and meant it.

Before the porridge cooled, a visiting aunt did it anyway.

Old Hungry Mist sent a crow to find out where things were kept.

Meanwhile, what one hears at a keyhole is soon known in a lair: the Mist learned it all.

The Mist came in disguise, holding out a sweet cake.

Well, Ivashka took the cake and fell asleep.

That night the Mist, a fog with teeth and an appetite for names, carried off Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands to the marsh where the road gave up.

Now the news went round, and at last it reached Ivashka.

The lad decided to set things right.

So Ivashka left home. Where three ways met, our Ivashka took the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

Under a dripping fir, Ivashka met an old woman knitting fog on a stump at the edge of the road. “Carry my bundle a while,” it said, and the trees leaned in to listen.

Ivashka answered politely.

An old woman knitting fog opened a sack that held three things: a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a jar of borrowed thunder. Before long an old woman knitting fog gave our Ivashka a bone comb that grows forests when thrown.

Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands was free.

And so, Ivashka went back the way he had come.

Behind came the Mist, chasing.

Ivashka flung the comb, and a forest sprang up thick as a brush, and escaped.

“Who else?,” said the elder brothers Glib and Gleb, and the court believed it.

One more trial was set: to find which of twelve identical doves was the princess.

The task was done by morning.

Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands looked twice and cried, “This is the one!”

The lie of the elder brothers Glib and Gleb came out.

As for the Mist: the Mist was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.

Ivashka and Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands ruled together, and lived long.
