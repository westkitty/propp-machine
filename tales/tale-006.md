# The Tale of Tikhon and Grandmother Ninetoes

*Seed 6. Functions: `α γ δ η θ A B C ↑ H J I K Q T U W`. Strict validation: PASS.*

Once, in a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays, lived Tikhon Who Counts Crows, who had never once finished counting.

Three days later Old father Foma sang it like a lullaby: do not eat the third pancake. The lad said “Of course”, already wondering.

Of course Tikhon Who Counts Crows did exactly what was forbidden.

Grandmother Ninetoes borrowed a face from a sleeping woman.

Our Tikhon believed every word.

That night, Ninetoes, a witch who lived in a hut that paced like a dog, swept in and carried off the Moonmiller’s daughter to her pacing hut.

“Who will go?” cried old father Foma. Only Tikhon stood up.

Our Tikhon put on his other boot and resolved to go.

At dawn, the lad went where his feet pointed. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, our Tikhon chose the road through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

There at the heart of the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences, Tikhon stood before the witch, a witch who lived in a hut that paced like a dog. “I am too stupid to understand," said our Tikhon, and Ninetoes, eager to show off, leaned over the edge.

In the fight, the witch scorched the lad across the brow: a mark that would matter later.

Ninetoes was beaten.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

The Moonmiller’s daughter saw the mark and cried, “This is the one!”

A crown that fitted, for once appeared for our Tikhon.

As for the witch: Ninetoes was tied to a mule’s tail and taken to a very long sermon.

So our Tikhon married the Moonmiller’s daughter, with pancakes stacked to the rafters.
