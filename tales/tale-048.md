# The Tale of Ivashka and Grandmother Ninetoes

Past three rivers and a rumour, in a village where the rooster crowed backwards, there was born Ivashka Ninth-Son, who slept on the stove for nineteen winters.

One day the hero’s grandmother went away to market and did not come back for a long time.

“Listen,” said the hero’s grandmother, “do not answer if someone calls your name from the forest.” Ivashka nodded, and began to wonder at once.

Forbidden things are heavy; Ivashka Ninth-Son could not stop carrying this one.

Then Grandmother Ninetoes came and stole the water of life and the water of death, and the whole land went dim.

The news went round, and at last it reached the lad.

Ivashka decided to set things right.

So the lad left home. Past the post with the rude carving, Ivashka chose the road that went through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything.

At a crossroads, our Ivashka met a drowned soldier who still kept watch beside a lake as flat as a coin. “Answer me this,” it said, and its eyes were very old.

Ivashka answered politely.

A drowned soldier who still kept watch unfolded a rag that held three things: a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a handkerchief that unfolds into a lake; boots that walk only forward. A drowned soldier who still kept watch gave Ivashka boots that walk only forward.

Wouldn’t you know it, Ivashka was led through the forest, where the trees whispered half of everything as far as the Birch Thicket of Unfinished Sentences.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

Our Ivashka went home.

Behind came Ninetoes, chasing.

At the last breath, the doll snatched Ivashka up.

The lad arrived home, but nobody knew him.

And Ninetoes: Ninetoes was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

Then there was a wedding.
