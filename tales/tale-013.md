# The Tale of Marfa and Old Hungry Mist

Once, in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it, lived Marfa Sootfoot, who could whistle in three directions at once.

On the eve of the fair, the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet packed three onions and a prayer and went away, and the house felt watched.

Three days later a one-eyed stranger came asking whose child that was. It was Old Hungry Mist.

A chattering neighbour told the Mist everything.

Not long after, That night, the fog, a fog with teeth and an appetite for names, came down the chimney and snatched away Tsarevna Lumina to the marsh where the road gave up.

“Is there anyone not busy?” cried the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet. Only Marfa stood up.

“I have nothing better to do, and I will go,” said Marfa.

Marfa set out along the road. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, Marfa took the road over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

There sat a drowned soldier who still kept watch beside a lake as flat as a coin, who asked the girl a question.

“Find your own crust,” said Marfa. A drowned soldier who still kept watch said nothing, which was worse.

Marfa was led over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud as far as the Thrice-Tenth Sea.

The cage swung open, and Tsarevna Lumina stepped out blinking.

Then, our Marfa went back the way she had come.

Behind came the fog, chasing.

A talkative skull with lantern eyes carried the girl away to safety.

Marfa arrived home, but nobody knew her.

The Captain of Mild Courage claimed the deed for himself.

The lie of the Captain of Mild Courage came out.

And the Mist was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.

There was a wedding, and the bells were rung so hard that one ran away.
