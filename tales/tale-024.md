# The Tale of Dunya and Count Moth

Past three rivers and a rumour, in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it, there once lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

Then the hero’s grandmother went off to sell a cow and a silence, and our Dunya was left alone with the stove.

Count Moth bottled up the water of life and the water of death, and the clocks ran backwards.

The hero’s grandmother wept loudly in the square, and Dunya heard.

Our Dunya set out along the road. At the crossroads stone, Dunya chose the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, Dunya stood before the lord, a lord made of dust and good manners. “Show me how it is done," said our Dunya, and the lord, eager to show off, leaned over the edge.

In the struggle, the Count scratched Dunya across the brow: a mark like a little moon.

And so the Count was defeated.

By the gift’s doing, the girl won back the water of life and the water of death.

Then, Dunya went back the way she had come.

Before long Dunya stepped through sheer stubbornness’s ear and out the other and came out handsome.

And the Count was turned into a draughty doorway that everyone complained about.
