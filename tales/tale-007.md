# The Tale of Dunya and Count Moth

*Seed 7. Functions: `α β ε ζ η θ A C ↑ K ↓ M N Q T W`. Strict validation: PASS.*

Once, in a village where the rooster crowed backwards, lived Dunya of the Leaky Bucket, whose bucket leaked only lies.

Then the village elder with the brass ear-trumpet set off on business and left Dunya alone.

At dusk, Count Moth listened.

What one hears at a keyhole is soon known in a lair: the Count learned it all.

The Count borrowed a face from a sleeping woman.

And Dunya took the cake and fell asleep.

The Count bottled up the egg in which a certain death was kept, and the clocks ran backwards.

“I have nothing better to do, and I will go,” said Dunya.

And the girl set off, it may be near, it may be far; the tale is quick, the road is not. When the road split like a lizard’s tongue, the girl took the road over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

Everything that had been taken was put right.

With the prize in her cap, Dunya turned for home.

The Tsaritsa of Nine Rivers set a task: to eat a whole oven of bread before dawn.

Before the candle was half gone the task was done, neatly.

Then Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands recognised Dunya.

A little wooden doll that ate porridge gave the girl new clothes.

Three days later Dunya and Tsarevich Lev Pale-Hands ruled together, and lived long.
