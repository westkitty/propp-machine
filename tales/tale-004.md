# The Tale of Yelena and Count Moth

Once, in a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it, lived Yelena Thimble-Eyes, who could see a needle think.

Well, a polite stranger came asking what the house was afraid of. It was Count Moth.

A chattering neighbour told the lord everything.

The Count bottled up the singing apple that told the truth, and the clocks ran backwards.

The hero’s grandmother called Yelena and said: “Go, and bring it back.”

“I will go,” said Yelena.

Well, Yelena set out along the road. At the crossroads stone, the girl chose the road that went down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

An old woman knitting fog on a stump at the edge of the road held out a cracked cup: “First a task, then a road.”

Our Yelena answered with a question and got it right on the third try.

An old woman knitting fog unfolded a rag that held three things: a bone comb that grows forests when thrown; a horse-hair that summons a horse of storm; a ball of red thread that knows the way. In return our Yelena received a ball of red thread that knows the way.

So Yelena came to the Copper Kingdom under the earth, having gone down a ladder of roots, deeper than the wells go.

There at the heart of the Copper Kingdom under the earth, Yelena stood before the Count, a lord made of dust and good manners. The lord challenged Yelena to a contest of riddles.

And so the Count was defeated.

Of course Yelena found the singing apple that told the truth again.

With the prize in her cap, the girl took the road back to a kingdom so small that the Tsar could hear every sneeze in it.

The Count flew after our Yelena.

Yelena let the red thread roll ahead, and it found the crooked path, and the lord was stuck behind it for three days.

At home nobody recognised Yelena.

The Moonmiller set a task: to sit in a red-hot bathhouse and come out cold.

With the cat’s help, the girl did it, and yawned.

By the deed, they knew the girl at last.

Yelena bathed in the milk of a white mare and came out handsome.

The Count was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.

The girl and the Moonmiller’s daughter ruled together, and lived long.
