# The Tale of Savva and Zmei Pickleback

They still tell of Savva the Turnip-Brave in a hut beside a river that ran uphill on Sundays: the one, who once outstared a turnip.

Then a widowed Tsar set off on business and left our Savva alone.

That night Zmei Pickleback, a salt-green serpent with three heads and one opinion, carried off the Moonmiller’s daughter to a cave that smelled of brine.

“Is there anyone not busy?” cried a widowed Tsar. Even the cat looked away.

Savva decided to set things right.

And our Savva went, long or short, high or low; the tale is quick, the road is not. At the crossroads stone, Savva took the road that went over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

Then, a drowned soldier who still kept watch beside a lake as flat as a coin barred the way: “First a task, then a road.”

Being stubborn, Savva bowed low.

A drowned soldier who still kept watch opened a sack that held three things: a jar of borrowed thunder; a birch-bark flute that makes the dead dance politely; boots that walk only forward. Of course a drowned soldier who still kept watch pulled from a sleeve boots that walk only forward. “Do not thank me yet.”

So Savva came to the Thrice-Tenth Sea, led over the sea, where the fish kept diaries and the waves read them aloud.

There at the heart of the Thrice-Tenth Sea, Savva stood before Pickleback. Pickleback set out two bowls and a riddle, and they matched wits until the candle drowned.

The Moonmiller’s daughter tied a ribbon round his wrist.

And so Pickleback was defeated.

Well, the cage swung open, and the Moonmiller’s daughter was free.

The lad went home.

Unknown, Savva took work in the royal kitchen and waited.

The Moonmiller stroked his beard. “Whoever can manage this: to find which of twelve identical doves was the princess.”

By luck and stubbornness, Savva did it before the cock crowed.

Everyone recognised Savva at last.

Nobody was sorry: Pickleback was made to count the crows of the kingdom, and is counting still.
