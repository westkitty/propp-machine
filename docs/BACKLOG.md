# Backlog: 15 next steps

1. **Interleaved moves.** Replace sequential move blocks with a stack or graph of open sequences, Bremond-style embedding and interleaving. Re-run *The Enchanted Tsarévich* and *The Grey Wolf*, which fail mainly because of this.
2. **A trebling and transposition generator mode.** Sample refined-valid tales with repeated D–E–F, mobile M–N and an early donor block, then measure whether transition entropy approaches the real tales' 1.88 bits (currently 0.92).
3. **Villain mode.** Play the villain's sphere (ε, ζ, η, A, H, Pr). The grammar requires that the villain loses, so the fun is in choosing how.
4. **ATU tale-type lookup.** Map each generated or annotated sequence to candidate ATU types (300, 301, 313, 327, 425, 480, 550, ...) from a small hand-built table of characteristic function patterns, and show "your tale resembles ATU 550" in the reader.
5. **Second annotator and agreement.** Have an independent person re-annotate the 12 tales blind and report Cohen's κ per function, following Finlayson's protocol.
6. **Bigger corpus.** Annotate all magic tales in the Magnus volume, then a public-domain Grimm set (Gutenberg #2591), and compare acceptance by collection.
7. **Annotation sensitivity.** For each judgment call (fields marked `note`), store the alternative coding and report the acceptance range, not a single number.
8. **Greimas view.** Add an actantial-model panel that folds the seven spheres into subject/object, sender/receiver and helper/opponent, and shows the roles a character occupies over a tale.
9. **Dundes mode.** A motifeme validator (Lack → Lack Liquidated; Interdiction → Violation → Consequence → Attempted Escape) to test North American and animal tales without Propp's full chain.
10. **Other folklore systems.** Campbell's monomyth, the Aarne-Thompson-Uther motif index (Thompson's *Motif-Index*), and Polti's 36 situations as alternative grammars over the same corpus, compared on acceptance.
11. **Branching map.** Show the whole space of choices as a fold-out map: every path the grammar would allow from the current page.
12. **Illustration motifs per character.** A generated woodcut portrait for each cast member, reused across pages, and illustrated borders that grow with the tale.
13. **Read aloud.** Optional speech synthesis with a storyteller's pauses at choice points, and an "audio book" export.
14. **Learn the probabilities.** Fit the generator's inclusion probabilities to the annotated corpus instead of hand-set values, and report the likelihood of held-out tales.
15. **Make the reader installable.** Add a manifest and service worker for offline use (it already runs from file://), and an export of a finished tale as a printable chapbook PDF.
