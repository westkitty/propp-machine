# What the Propp Machine taught us

Propp's *Morphology* reads like a machine specification: 31 functions, a fixed order, seven roles, and "moves" that restart the cycle. It is very tempting to type it in. We did, three ways: as data a validator can check, as a sampler that writes new tales, and as a book a reader can steer. Each version shows something different about the theory.

**As a generator, Propp works almost too well.** If you sample functions in canonical order and close every pair, you get something that feels like a wonder tale: interdiction, violation, villainy, a strange donor at a crossroads, a gift, a fight, a homecoming, a wedding. A thousand seeds gave 988 distinct sequences, and every one of them passed the strict validator. Propp's grammar is sufficient for producing tale-like plots. That is roughly Gervás's point too: as a plot driver it holds up. Interactivity also fits naturally. Propp's functions are slots, and a reader's choice only changes how a slot is filled: rude or kind to the donor, the forest or the sea, sword or riddle. Because the sampler can only continue along permitted paths, the tale stays valid whatever the reader does. A choice at the donor's test that denies the hero a gift doesn't break the story. It changes which kind of victory remains available.

**As a description of real tales, the same grammar is strikingly brittle.** Read literally, it accepted none of our twelve tales from Afanas'ev, not even the nine magic tales. With Propp's own allowances added (trebling, implicit functions, the mobile task, inverted reconnaissance) and Bremond's unrealised possibilities, it accepted two. The pairings mostly held. What failed was the order. Real tellers hand over the magic doll at a deathbed before the story's first villainy. They let the wolf issue fresh prohibitions in the middle of a quest, and they leave one villainy open while another runs. Propp drew his sequence from a corpus. It describes the genre's centre of gravity, not every telling, and he said as much when he discussed trebling and transposition. Our acceptance rate measures how far individual tellings sit from that centre.

**The failures fall into three groups.** Some tales are outside Propp's type: an animal fable, a lawsuit novella, a prophecy tale. They don't nearly fit; they don't fit at all. That is the honest result Propp's own scope predicts, and it is why Dundes had to cut the model down to a handful of motifemes before it could travel to North America. Some failures are structural, and Bremond diagnosed them in 1966: stories embed and interleave sequences, and any opening may fail to close. Zamorýshek never wins his brides; the witch's devoured family is never restored. Some failures are ours: where to place a false claim made offstage while the hero lies dead is an annotator's decision, not a fact in the text. Finlayson's ProppLearner project spent years and serious money on double annotation for exactly this reason. One reader, even with every quotation checked by script, is not a measurement of agreement.

**What the machine cannot see is what the tales are about.** The stone Shemyák's defendant holds behind his back, and the nightingale's prophecy that a son will be served by his parents, have no function codes. Yet they are the plots. Lévi-Strauss objected that Propp described form by throwing away content. In a validator the objection becomes concrete: every X in the corpus is a piece of meaning the grammar has no word for.

So the grammar is a good loom and a poor census. It can weave endless valid tales. It can also tell a real tale where it sits away from the genre's centre, and that is more interesting than a pass mark. It does not capture all folktales, or even all of Afanas'ev's. Its usefulness comes from making each mismatch precise enough to argue about.
